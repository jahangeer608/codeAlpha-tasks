      Summary Report

      1. Overview & Methodology

      2. Key Findings

      • Strongest area: Team collaboration (avg 4.4/5) — employees consistently feel their teams work well together.
      • Second strongest: Psychological safety (avg 4.0/5) — most employees feel comfortable sharing ideas and opinions.
      • Weakest areas: Work–life balance and recognition (avg 2.8/5 each) — the two lowest-scoring dimensions, flagged independently by multiple respondents in open comments.
      • Growth opportunities and internal communication also scored below the overall average (3.0/5 each), suggesting employees want clearer paths forward and more visibility into decisions.
      • General job satisfaction and feeling valued both averaged 3.8/5 — solidly positive but with room to grow.

      3. Strengths to Protect

      • Collaborative team culture — a genuine asset that shows up in both scores and comments.
      • Openness to employee voice — people feel safe contributing ideas, which supports innovation and trust.

      4. Areas for Improvement

      • Recognition — introduce regular, lightweight recognition (peer shout-outs, manager check-ins) rather than relying only on formal reviews.
      • Work–life balance — review after-hours workload expectations, particularly for roles that scored lowest.
      • Career growth — publish clearer promotion criteria and create individual development conversations each quarter.
      • Communication — establish a consistent channel (e.g. monthly update) for sharing decisions that affect employees.

      5. Recommendations

      • Pilot a peer-recognition program over the next quarter and re-measure the recognition score.
      • Set a manager expectation for one growth-focused conversation per employee per quarter.
      • Introduce a monthly all-hands or written update to close the communication gap.
      • Re-run this survey in 3–6 months with a larger sample to track whether these actions move the score.

      6. Conclusion

      Engagement is moderately positive, anchored by strong collaboration and a culture where people feel heard. The clearest opportunities are recognition, work–life balance, and growth visibility — all addressable through low-cost, consistent management practices rather than structural change. A follow-up survey with a larger sample is recommended to validate these findings at scale.
